Selected Work

Two pieces of coursework exploring how we understand the past and the society we live in.

History, Memory, and the Classroom

In my Legacies of War paper, I examine how public memory shapes our understanding of history. Drawing on examples from the Civil War, the Lost Cause, and the U.S.–Dakota War, I consider whose perspectives are represented and how students can evaluate historical narratives.

Critical thinkingSource synthesisLesson planning

This paper reflects a shift in my thinking: learning history means examining both what happened and how people remember it. I apply that insight in a proposed classroom lesson built around evidence and inquiry.

A Sociological Analysis of Parasite

This paper examines the film Parasite through the theories of Karl Marx, Thorstein Veblen, and Émile Durkheim. It connects the film’s class conflict and economic inequality to exploitation, conspicuous consumption and leisure, organic solidarity, and anomie.

Film analysisApplying social theoryComparative analysis

This paper demonstrates my ability to apply abstract sociological ideas to a contemporary film. Comparing three theorists helped me connect economic inequality, displays of status, and weakened social bonds to specific characters and events.
